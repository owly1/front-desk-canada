# Prospective customers and discovery plan

Research/access date: **September 26, 2026**. All 30 organizations below are **uncontacted and unqualified prospects**. Their existence and relevant offerings are supported by the linked organizational pages; language friction, budget and purchase interest are unverified.

Public routes are organizational contacts, not permission to send messages. No outreach was sent. Recheck details immediately before any authorized outreach. Buyer roles are proposed roles to locate, not verified purchasing authority.

## Fields and qualification rules

- **Languages: unknown for every organization below.** English-language websites do not establish customer language demand. Select pilot languages from actual inquiry records and fluent reviewers.
- **Current booking/PMS/CRM systems and API permissions: unknown unless stated.** “Integration need” below describes a proposed workflow, not installed software.
- **P1:** first discovery cohort; **P2:** second cohort/backup; **P3:** useful comparator or extra qualification required. These ranks are analyst judgments.
- Proposed needs: **B** = tour availability, booking confirmation and changes; **H** = hotel reception/reservation context; **G** = group inquiry routing; **R** = remote guest-service continuity. A concept video can simulate these; a pilot must establish actual permissions and failure behavior.

## First ten discovery conversations

Recommended order: **Waterloo Central Railway → Toronto Harbour Tours → Go Tours Canada → Toronto Bicycle Tours → Tasty Tours → Tour Guys → Niagara Day Tour → Toronto EcoAdventures → Walper → Cambridge Hotel & Conference Centre.**

This sequence combines locally accessible discovery, different tour-booking workflows, and two hotel comparisons. It is not evidence that these organizations have the strongest language need. Ask for the relevant staff member before assuming the listed role owns the problem.

### First three conditional pilots

| Candidate | Why investigate first | Conditions before offering a pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Waterloo Central Railway | Local visitor setting; concrete ticketing task | Enough qualifying interactions, available staff champion, budget authority despite charitable/volunteer structure, suitable supported language pair |
| Toronto Harbour Tours | Clear ticket-desk story, service selection and party-size details | Actual counter access and noise assessment, language demand, integration scope, participation outside operational/safety instructions |
| Go Tours Canada | Owner/operations access and bounded group/booking questions | A suitable staffed touchpoint, feasible service-language support after booking, enough volume and willingness to compare against current tools |

If those conditions fail, move to Walper or Cambridge Hotel instead of calling a weak-fit trial a success. These are invitations to qualify, not promised partners.

## Interview guide: test behavior and economics

1. Tell us about the most recent customer conversation you could not comfortably complete in a shared language.
2. What was the customer trying to buy or change? What actually happened next?
3. Show the current workflow using a fictional example. Which tools or colleagues did you use?
4. How many comparable incidents occurred last week and last month? Is there a record?
5. Which languages and channels occur in those incidents? Who can validate a translation?
6. What did the workaround cost in time, fees, refunds or genuinely lost contribution profit?
7. When does your existing translator work well? Why would you keep it?
8. Can the customer receive the full service after a translated booking? Where would specialist language help still be required?
9. Who approves software, what systems must it connect to, and what did your last similar purchase involve?
10. After seeing the concept, would you commit staff time to a comparison pilot and discuss a paid continuation? What evidence would you need first?

Ask about past behavior before showing the video. Otherwise polished screens can elicit enthusiasm without establishing demand.

## Evidence that changes or rejects the customer hypothesis

- Most interviewed operators cannot recall recurring qualifying incidents, or fewer than roughly five occur per week. That frequency is an initial screening hypothesis, not a universal break-even rule; high-value cases may differ.
- Customers complete bookings just as well with a free translator, and staff see no meaningful benefit from confirmation or handoff.
- The underlying experience cannot be delivered in the customer's language, so improved booking merely shifts the problem downstream.
- Operators cannot establish enough incremental contribution or useful capacity to cover the subscription.
- Staff avoid the tool during real shifts; setup, noise or handover breaks adoption.
- Support/integration requires recurring bespoke labor that the proposed price cannot cover.
- No decision-maker will approve a paid continuation after a useful, fairly evaluated trial.

The ambitious video helps explain the future experience. The interviews determine whether that experience deserves to become a business.
