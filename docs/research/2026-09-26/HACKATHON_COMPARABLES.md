# Winning projects and pitch comparisons

Research date: **September 26, 2026**. Six projects below have verified awards; two additional projects are closer technical references with **unverified award status**. None of the six is a verified exact match for a commercial human-to-human business interpreter. Awards demonstrate a competition result, not revenue or product-market fit.

No original slide deck was located for these projects. Three original public video transcripts were inspected: Dealwise, AMUSH and Rosetta. Those transcripts are automatically generated; gaps and transcription errors limit conclusions about exact speech and visual execution. Other demos are linked but their full narrative was not independently inspected. We do not know whether each linked recording exactly matches what judges saw on stage.

### 1. GibberLink: one instantly recognizable change

**Product and user:** two AI phone agents identify each other and switch from spoken language to a machine-oriented audio protocol, GGWave. This is agent-to-agent communication, not translation between human languages. ElevenLabs supplies the voice-agent component; GGWave is a distinct part of the mechanism. No validated business model or paying-customer evidence was located in the reviewed artifacts. [Original project](https://devpost.com/software/gibber-link)

**Distinctive idea:** the audible protocol switch makes an otherwise invisible technical choice perceptible. A familiar phone interaction creates context for a surprising change. The organizer describes unanimous judging and a compelling viral demonstration; that is documented commentary. The inference for us is that an observable transition can be memorable even without a long architecture explanation. [Award announcement](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/announcing-the-winners-of-the-elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon)

**Assessment:** strong conceptual novelty and demonstration identity; commercial value and production reliability remain unverified here. Actual opening wording, ending ask and stage slides were not inspected. Our reconstruction would be “ordinary call → recognition → protocol switch → explain benefit,” not a claim about an original deck.

**Borrow:** make the moment when the shared booking updates after a correction immediately visible. **Do not borrow:** unrelated machine-speech spectacle or a claim that it solves multilingual human service.

### 2. Procuro: business work made concrete

**Product and user:** procurement teams facing supplier coordination. The submission describes searching suppliers, making calls, comparing information, and handling purchasing/logistics work using ElevenLabs, Twilio and other tools. This is an autonomous task agent. Monetization and actual enterprise adoption were not established. [Submission](https://elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon.devpost.com/submissions/622443-procuro)

**Distinction:** telephone conversations are attached to a recognizable business workflow rather than presented as generic chat. The organizer specifically discusses its demonstration calling US suppliers about delayed parts. This supports a demo-quality observation, not proof of purchasing accuracy or production integration. [Organizer](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/announcing-the-winners-of-the-elevenlabs-worldwide-hackathon)

**Assessment:** business problem and task framing are more relevant to us than technical novelty alone. Repository availability and end-to-end implementation could not be independently checked. No exact hook, timing, visual sequence or closing ask is asserted because the original video was not fully inspected.

**Borrow:** let judges see the agreed transaction status at the end. **Avoid:** inventing supplier/customer adoption or suggesting a staged screen proves a real external transaction.

## What this evidence means for our video

1. **Make the task legible within ten seconds.** Dealwise is the clearest observed example.
2. **Use the language feature to resolve a specific question.** AMUSH supplies the closest commercial presentation reference.
3. **Introduce one correction.** Updating the right state shows more than a perfect greeting.
4. **Produce something durable.** Rosetta's notes suggest the value of our bilingual confirmation and handoff context.
5. **Give the audience one memorable transition.** GibberLink illustrates the power of a clearly perceptible change; our equivalent is two different languages resolving into one agreed transaction.
6. **Separate pitch strength from business proof.** No comp here supplies validated unit economics for our product.

These are analytical lessons, not causal proof of what makes judges vote. For an ambitious concept video, borrow the narrative clarity while clearly stating what is staged. The actual AF Hacks criteria include viability, so show a credible first buyer and validation plan alongside the vision.
