## Suggested narration

“Imagine a small plumbing team midway through a job. Another customer needs an appointment, but the owner is busy and may not share the caller's preferred language. The request becomes another callback and another piece of coordination.

“Front Desk Canada is a prototype for turning that inquiry into a clear next step. Our first customer hypothesis is a small plumbing or HVAC operator in Waterloo Region that handles recurring phone inquiries.

“Here is the workflow using a fictional business and local demo calendar. We collect the service details, use the business's approved price ranges, and ask the caller to confirm before reserving a slot. The owner gets a persistent record. Requests outside the approved workflow go to human review.

“ElevenLabs is our planned voice interface. The booking rules and records live in our application. [Replace this sentence with a precise account of the live integration only after verification.]

“Jobber and Canadian bilingual receptionists already serve this market. Our hypothesis is that focused local setup and verified multilingual handoffs can reduce the owner's coordination work.

“We propose testing C$249 per month with bounded usage. The next step is three to five pilots measuring correct bookings, owner handling time, full delivery costs and willingness to renew.

“For Growing Canada, the outcome we want to prove is practical: the same small team serving customers with less coordination. We're looking for introductions to three local operators to test it.”

## Five-minute submission version

The [current event page](https://luma.com/asvdo3m9) allows up to five minutes and asks for purpose, user value, and a feature walkthrough. Use a roughly two-minute core story, then a clearly labeled feature segment: owner review/audit, booking persistence, human-review path, unsent SMS preview, and the simulated multilingual Campaign Studio. End with economics and pilot evidence sought. This lets the short pitch remain focused while the longer submission covers the app's features.

Suggested allocation: 30 seconds problem/buyer; 100 seconds workflow; 50 seconds remaining features and status; 40 seconds competition; 40 seconds economics/validation; 20 seconds ask. Total 4 minutes 40 seconds, leaving room for transitions.

## What winning comparables suggest

Procuro makes supplier coordination tangible; Dealwise makes collected quotes visible; AMUSH ties languages to a seller's workflow; GibberLink creates an immediately noticeable change. Junction is especially relevant because the useful output is an appointment and contextual handoff. These lessons are interpretation of public submissions and official award reports, not a reconstruction of their slide order or private judging feedback.

Our equivalent demonstration should let a judge see the caller's request become a correct owner record. The strongest additional evidence before judging would be a real consented provider conversation, an independently checked language example, and one actual discovery interview. Report any of these only after they occur.

## Business questions to rehearse

1. Who pays? The operator; callers use the service without buying an app.
2. Why pay? Measurable handling time saved and correctly completed inquiries; willingness to pay remains untested.
3. Why not Jobber? It is a strong alternative, particularly for existing customers. Our proposed advantage must be demonstrated in a narrower workflow.
4. Why ElevenLabs? Voice is central to the intended caller experience; it still requires verified integration. The vendor does not give us exclusivity.
5. What is the moat? None proven. Domain evaluation, integrations, onboarding and trusted distribution are potential assets to build.
6. How profitable? The base planning case leaves C$120/account/month before acquisition and company overhead; real usage and support costs may change that substantially.
7. What would make you change direction? Weak call volume, little language demand, no paid renewal, or support costs that overwhelm the price.
8. What are you asking for? Three qualified operator introductions and feedback on a bounded pilot.
